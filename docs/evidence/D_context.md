## 3. Australian housing policy 2025–2026: what is legislated and what is proposed

### 3.1 Negative gearing and the CGT discount: LEGISLATED
- Announced in the 2026–27 Budget, delivered 12 May 2026 by Treasurer Jim Chalmers.
- Bill: Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Bill 2026. Introduced to the House on 28 May 2026. Senate Economics Legislation Committee hearings 15–16 June; the committee reported on 19 June recommending passage. Passed the Senate with Greens support on 25 June 2026, and the House agreed to the Senate amendments. Baker McKenzie, Jul 2026: https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/australia-major-changes-to-cgt-and-negative-gearing ; Broker News, 25 Jun 2026: https://www.brokernews.com.au/news/breaking-news/negative-gearing-and-cgt-overhaul-becomes-law-289572.aspx
- Enacted as the Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026, No. 49 of 2026 (C2026A00049). The Federal Register page carries a date of 26 June 2026, but the page does not label it as the assent date. https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00049
- Design as enacted (Baker McKenzie):
  - **CGT:** from 1 July 2027 the 50% discount is removed for individuals, partnerships and trusts and replaced by cost-base indexation. A 30% minimum tax applies to net capital gains. Assets are deemed disposed of and reacquired on 1 July 2027, so gains accrued before that date keep the discount. Owners of new dwellings and affordable housing may choose between the discount and indexation.
  - **Negative gearing:** from 1 July 2027, residential dwellings acquired after 7:30pm AEST on 12 May 2026 can offset net rental losses only against residential property income. New dwellings and dwellings acquired before 12 May 2026 are exempt.
- Greens-negotiated amendments: a ban on SMSF limited-recourse borrowing for residential property, and a rule that any future winding back of the reforms requires legislation rather than regulation (Real Estate Business). https://www.realestatebusiness.com.au/industry/32071-federal-government-passes-property-tax-reforms
- **Not yet legislated (proposed or pending):** the 30% minimum tax on discretionary trust distributions from 1 July 2028; the Innovative Business CGT Concession (announced 18 June 2026); a tranche-2 fix for grandfathering on death or divorce transfers of jointly owned property; small-business carve-outs.
- Government and Treasury estimates (Aussie, 13 May 2026; Broker News): about 75,000 more home owners over a decade; house price growth about 2% lower over a couple of years; $3.6bn extra revenue over the forward estimates (about 85% from negative gearing); more than $40bn over a decade. https://www.aussie.com.au/insights/articles/federal-budget-2026-property-tax-changes/

## 5. Housing–fertility causal evidence

1. **Dettling, L. J. & Kearney, M. S. (2014). "House prices and birth rates: The impact of the real estate market on the decision to have a baby." *Journal of Public Economics* 110: 82–100. DOI 10.1016/j.jpubeco.2013.09.009.** Uses MSA-level house price variation across 66 MSAs, 1990–2006, for women aged 20–44. In the average MSA, a 10% rise in house prices is associated with about a **4.5% increase in births among owners** (home-equity effect) and about a **1% decrease among non-owners** (price effect). The negative effect is larger for subsequent births and for women over 30. NBER Digest: https://www.nber.org/digest/feb12/w17485.html ; UMD: https://www.econ.umd.edu/publication/house-prices-and-birth-rates-impact-real-estate-market-decision-have-baby
2. **Lovenheim, M. F. & Mumford, K. J. (2013). "Do family wealth shocks affect fertility choices? Evidence from the housing market." *Review of Economics and Statistics* 95(2): 464–475.** Uses PSID data. A $100,000 increase in housing wealth among owners raises the probability of a birth by **16–18%**. There is **no detectable effect for renters** from MSA price growth. https://ideas.repec.org/a/tpr/restat/v95y2013i2p464-475.html
3. **Kearney, M. S., Levine, P. B. & Pardue, L. (2022). "The Puzzle of Falling US Birth Rates since the Great Recession." *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 36(1): 151–176. DOI 10.1257/jep.36.1.151.** The US birth rate fell from 69.1 per 1,000 women aged 15–44 (2007) to 58.3 (2019) and 55.8 (2020), about a 20% drop. Ten state-level economic and policy variables explain only about **6.2%** of the decline. The authors find **no negative association between state rent increases and birth-rate changes** (a null result for housing). Childcare costs, student debt and LARC also show null or near-zero associations. The authors attribute the decline speculatively to cohort-level "shifting priorities." https://econ.umd.edu/sites/www.econ.umd.edu/files/pubs/jep.36.1.151.pdf
4. **Kearney, M. S. & Levine, P. B. (2025). "Why Is Fertility So Low in High Income Countries?" NBER WP 33989.** Childlessness at age 30 is near or above 50% for the youngest cohorts in every country studied. The authors conclude the decline reflects "shifting priorities" and the smaller role of parenthood in adult life plans. Incremental financial or family policies have at most small effects. The NBER Digest summary does not discuss housing. NBER Digest, Oct 2025: https://www.nber.org/digest/202510/declining-fertility-wealthy-nations ; https://ideas.repec.org/p/nbr/nberwo/33989.html
5. **Atalay, K., Li, A. & Whelan, S. (2017). "Housing Wealth and Fertility: Australian Evidence." University of Sydney Economics WP 2017-08** (later published; journal details not verified). Uses HILDA data with regional house price variation. For owners, a $100,000 rise in house prices raises the probability of a birth by **7.5%** and fertility intentions by 2.5%. The effect is concentrated among married women in their early 30s who already have a child. https://ideas.repec.org/p/syd/wpaper/2017-08.html
6. **Clark, J. & Ferrer, A. (2019). "The effect of house prices on fertility: Evidence from Canada." *Economics: The Open-Access, Open-Assessment E-Journal* 13 (2019-38).** Lagged house prices have a positive effect on marginal and possibly completed fertility for owners, confined to non-movers. No comparable positive effect was reported for renters. Effect sizes were not given on the summary page. https://crdcn.ca/publication/the-effect-of-house-prices-on-fertility-evidence-from-canada/

Synthesis: across US, Australian and Canadian micro-studies, house price increases raise fertility among owners through a wealth effect, while the price effect on renters is negative and small (about −1% per 10% price rise in Dettling–Kearney) or undetectable (Lovenheim–Mumford; Kearney–Levine–Pardue state-level rents). The leading US decomposition attributes little of the post-2007 decline to measurable economic factors, housing included.

## 6. Rural–urban

Metro unemployment was not given in the report. https://ers.usda.gov/media/20831/eib-295.pdf

## Caveats
1. **Age bands differ:** the US and UK use 16–24; Korea's national youth definition is 15–29; India's is 15–29; South Africa's headline is 15–34; China uses 16–24 urban excluding students; the Eurostat series covers under-25s.
2. **Secondary reports:** several national figures (Korea, China, Indonesia, Canada, the Eurostat country rates, the UK unemployment rate, Australia TFR, Japan TFR) were taken from news or industry reports of official releases. Verify against the primary release before publication.
3. **London conflict in Demographia:** the 2026 PDF extraction gave Greater London as 8.5, while The Standard reported London at 6.9. Check the PDF table directly.
4. **Cotality report partner:** the Nov 2025 report is Cotality–ANU (the series was previously ANZ–CoreLogic). Deposit-saving times differ between the all-dwellings (11.0 years) and houses-only (12 years) measures.
5. **Australian ownership 2021, age 25–34:** the 43% figure is from The Conversation, not an ABS or AIHW table. The 1981 figure (61.1%) and the 2016 figure (44.6%) are AIHW census analysis.
6. **India PLFS 2025** uses a new sampling design and is not strictly comparable with earlier rounds.
7. **US CDC 2025** data are provisional, and the TFR was not shown in the retrieved text.
8. **Brookings "distressed counties"** are defined by income and employment, not by metro or nonmetro status. Micropolitan areas are a partial proxy for non-metro.
9. **Rhodium energy communities** are IRA-defined and not equivalent to rural areas.
